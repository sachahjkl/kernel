The heron at the head of the home of the site had seven animations and stood still most of the time. It now has fifteen, and it is still for about 40 percent of the time. In the dash, the heron squashes, runs out of the window by its right edge, comes back by its left edge, goes past its place, squashes on the stop and stands again. The water stays where it is. The other new animations: a hop, a fish that jumps in front of the heron, a doze with a letter z of squares, a stretch, the wind in the crest, a change of the leg it stands on, and a flap of the wing. Each animation plays once before one plays again, in an order that chance gives, and none plays two times in a row. The page keeps what was played for the length of the visit, so a reader who comes back to the home sees another animation. A reader whose system asks for reduced motion sees a heron that does not move at all. Nothing plays in a hidden tab or while the reader types in the search. Each frame is still made of the square cells of the mark: a squash or a stretch changes the width and the height of the whole picture, with sharp pixels.
