The heron of the home is pixel art: a grid of square cells. When it squashed before a dash or stretched in a hop, the page made its whole picture wider or higher, and the cells became rectangles. That is gone. A cell of the heron is now a square of one size in every picture. A squashed heron is another drawing of the heron in the same cells: the crouch before the dash is 28 cells wide and 19 high, where the heron at rest is 24 by 22. Four columns of the body are drawn two times, and two rows of the neck and one of the legs are left out. The head, the eye, the beak and the crest are the same cells in each of the ten forms. The leg that the heron stands on stays above its foot. Two forms are corrected by hand, cell by cell: a leg of the run that was two cells thick, two feet that joined, a tail too far from the body. The heron also travels by whole cells. In the dash it goes more than ten cells between two pictures of the screen, and it is never between two cells. The page changes the size of no picture.
