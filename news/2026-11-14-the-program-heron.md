The system has a program named `heron`. Its window shows the heron of the mark, drawn large from its square cells, in its water. The heron plays its fifteen animations by the rule of the home of the site: each one once in a round, in an order that chance gives, and it is still for about 40 percent of the time. In the dash the heron runs out of the window by the right and comes back by the left. A squash or a stretch is one of the drawn forms of the heron, in whole square cells, the same drawings as on the site, and the heron travels by whole cells. The key `g` draws the same moves in a free way, where the frame is stretched and its cells become rectangles, so a reader can compare the two. The keys `1` to `9` and `a` to `f` play one animation at once. Space pauses, and the dot shows the next frame. The key `l` shows the lines between the cells and a white outline around each cell that a frame changes: an animation changes a few cells. The picture shows the dash held at its third frame, in the form of the run. The window draws again only the part that changed, and the log gives the time and the damage of a picture as measures.
