The key super now opens the palette, which replaces the launcher. It is one panel with one list: the programs that the system can start, and the windows that are open. With no letter typed, it shows the windows first, then the programs. Each typed letter narrows the list. The match is the fuzzy match of the console: the letters appear in a name in their order, not always side by side, and a line marks each matched letter. Each row says what it is in words: a window, a program, the close of a window. Enter starts the chosen program or brings the chosen window to the front. The close of a window asks for a second Enter, on a red row that says what stops. The typed text is at twice the size of the font, in a band that touches the top, the left and the right edge of the panel, with no frame around it. The pointer works too: a move chooses a row, a press runs it. The panel has no right of its own: it starts a program as the menu of the bar does.
